# Effects of Ride-Sharing Employment on Educational Outcomes: Balancing Work and Study Among Private University Student Drivers in Dhaka

## Overview

This study examines how ride-sharing employment affects the academic and financial outcomes of private university students in Dhaka. Platforms such as Uber and Pathao provide flexible income that can help students pay tuition and living expenses, but intensive work may reduce study time and increase physical and mental fatigue.

The study focuses particularly on whether working **more than 5 hours per day** is associated with poorer academic outcomes and whether short-term earnings may create longer-term costs through delayed graduation.

## Research Objectives

The study aims to:

* Examine the relationship between ride-sharing work hours and academic performance.
* Determine whether intensive work reduces study time and increases academic disruption.
* Assess the relationship between work intensity and graduation delays.
* Examine physical and mental fatigue among student drivers.
* Identify the financial factors that encourage students to enter ride-sharing employment.
* Compare short-term ride-sharing income with potential long-term educational and earnings costs.

## Theoretical Framework

The study is based on a simple time-allocation model:

$$
T = A + H + S + L
$$

where:

* $T$ = total available time
* $A$ = academic/institutional time
* $H$ = ride-sharing work
* $S$ = study time
* $L$ = leisure, sleep and other activities

As work hours increase, study and recovery time may decline:

$$
\frac{\partial S}{\partial H}<0
$$

This may lead to academic disruption and, in some cases, delayed graduation.

### Intertemporal Financial Trade-Off

The study compares current ride-sharing income with potential long-term costs:

$$
NPV = PV(\text{Ride-sharing Income})
- \text{Additional Tuition}
- PV(\text{Foregone Graduate Earnings})
$$

A negative NPV would suggest that, under the model's assumptions, the long-term financial costs exceed the short-term benefits.

## Research Hypotheses

**H1:** Students working more than 5 hours per day are more likely to report academic deterioration.

**H2:** Higher work intensity is associated with lower study time.

**H3:** Higher work intensity is associated with greater physical and mental fatigue.

**H4:** Higher work intensity is associated with a greater likelihood of delayed graduation.

**H5:** Greater financial pressure is associated with higher ride-sharing work intensity.

## Methodology

The study uses a **quantitative cross-sectional survey** of:

* **40 male student drivers**
* **15 private universities**
* **Dhaka**

Analysis includes:

* Descriptive statistics
* Chi-square/Fisher's exact tests
* Ordinal logistic regression, where appropriate
* Thematic analysis of open-ended responses

Due to the small, non-random sample, the findings should be interpreted as **associations rather than definitive causal effects**.

## Core Economic Argument

The study investigates whether the following trade-off exists:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Short-Term Ride-Sharing Income}
\quad vs. \quad
\text{Academic + Long-Term Financial Costs}
}
$$

The central question is not whether student employment is inherently harmful, but whether **high-intensity employment becomes economically counterproductive when its academic and long-term opportunity costs exceed its immediate financial benefits.**

## Limitations

* Small sample size ($n=40$)
* Non-random sampling
* Cross-sectional design
* Self-reported data
* Potential selection and reporting bias

Therefore, the study identifies **relationships and patterns**, rather than proving that ride-sharing directly causes lower academic performance.
