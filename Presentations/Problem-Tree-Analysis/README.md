# Empowering Divorced and Single Mothers in Bangladesh: A Problem Tree Analysis

## Overview

This development framework uses **Problem Tree Analysis** to examine the structural, economic, social, and institutional barriers affecting divorced and single mothers in Bangladesh.

The analysis maps how factors such as **social stigma, limited employment opportunities, financial exclusion, legal barriers, and inadequate childcare** can contribute to economic vulnerability and social exclusion.

Based on these relationships, the project proposes policy interventions focused on **economic self-reliance, legal protection, financial inclusion, childcare support, and social acceptance**.

## Research Objectives

* Identify key socio-economic and institutional barriers affecting self-reliance.
* Examine the root causes behind economic and social marginalization.
* Map the potential consequences for mothers, children, and communities.
* Develop targeted policy responses addressing the identified barriers.

## Problem Tree Framework

### Root Causes

**Education & Skills**

* Limited access to education and vocational training.
* Early marriage and school dropout can restrict future employment opportunities.

**Labour Market**

* Potential discrimination against divorced and single mothers.
* Caregiving responsibilities can limit employment options and career progression.

**Financial Exclusion**

* Limited access to formal credit, savings, and business finance.
* Collateral and guarantor requirements can create barriers to financial access.

**Social & Cultural Norms**

* Social attitudes that associate women's status and identity with marriage.
* Stigma surrounding divorced and single-parent households.

**Legal & Institutional Barriers**

* Difficulties accessing property, alimony, and child-support entitlements.
* Complex, costly, or time-consuming legal procedures.

**Childcare**

* Limited availability of affordable and reliable childcare.
* Insufficient community-level support for working mothers.

### Core Problem

**Economic Vulnerability and Social Exclusion of Divorced and Single Mothers**

The interaction of these barriers can restrict women's ability to achieve **economic independence, financial security, and social inclusion**.

### Potential Effects

* Economic insecurity
* Limited income and wealth accumulation
* Social exclusion and stigma
* Reduced employment opportunities
* Greater vulnerability for dependent children
* Potential intergenerational poverty

## Proposed Policy Interventions

* **Skills Development:** Expand vocational and technical training tailored to women's employment opportunities.
* **Inclusive Employment:** Strengthen workplace protections and encourage inclusive hiring practices.
* **Financial Inclusion:** Provide accessible credit, savings, and entrepreneurship financing with flexible requirements.
* **Social Awareness:** Use community, media, and educational initiatives to challenge stigma and discriminatory attitudes.
* **Legal Support:** Improve access to legal aid and strengthen the enforcement of relevant rights.
* **Childcare Support:** Expand affordable community-based childcare through public and private-sector partnerships.

## Key Development Insights

1. **Childcare affects economic participation.**
   Limited childcare can restrict a mother's ability to participate in paid employment.

2. **Financial inclusion can strengthen self-reliance.**
   Access to appropriate credit, savings, and business finance can improve women's ability to generate and manage income.

3. **Legal rights require accessible enforcement.**
   Formal rights have limited practical value when legal procedures are difficult or costly to access.

4. **Social norms can influence economic outcomes.**
   Stigma and gender norms can affect employment opportunities, financial access, and participation in community life.

## Analytical Approach

The project applies **Problem Tree Analysis** to connect underlying causes with their consequences and potential solutions.

* **Root Causes** → Cultural, economic, legal, institutional, and labour-market factors
* **Core Problem** → Economic vulnerability and social exclusion
* **Effects** → Financial insecurity, limited opportunities, and social exclusion
* **Interventions** → Targeted policy and institutional responses
